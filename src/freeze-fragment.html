The Thursday freeze: three ways to lay it out

Screen 3. On Thursday at 10:00 the next week is agreed and locked. The frozen list is what SMRP 5.4.3 and 5.4.4 (Schedule Compliance) measure against, and anything worked later that was not in it is a break-in. SAP cannot hold this snapshot for us today (the exports carry neither Revision nor user status), so the freeze lives in the tool. Example: week 39, frozen Thursday 17 Sep 2026 during the planning meeting.

A

Readiness gate: a checklist that must be clean before the button unlocks

The freeze is a gate. The tool checks the week and lists what blocks it (red), what is allowed but must be seen (gold) and what is fine (green). The Freeze button stays off while a red item exists. Once frozen, the week shows a lock with the time and the snapshot becomes the reference for the KPIs.

  1. Open next week (39) on Thursday morning. The tool runs the checks: every row has a SAP order, every order has estimated hours, parts status, crew load, orders without the SAP week marker.
  2. Red items block: fix them in SAP (create the order, put the hours) and re-upload, or take the row out of the week.
  3. Gold items do not block: planned overtime, parts not confirmed, a job on the same day as a down day. They are shown so the meeting sees them.
  4. Press Freeze. The tool stores the snapshot: orders, hours per day, crew, capacity. The week gets a lock badge with date, time and who froze it.
  5. The meeting summary (list by area, week headline, overtime lines) is generated from the snapshot, ready to project or print.
  6. Until Sunday night the planner can unfreeze and freeze again with a reason; from Monday, every change is a break-in or a deferral with a reason.

B

The meeting screen: the summary tab as a slide, frozen at the end of the meeting

The freeze is the last click of the Thursday meeting. The screen is what gets projected: week headline, down days, the list by area with order numbers and hours, crew load, and the open points. Changes agreed in the room are made on the spot; when everyone agrees, the planner presses Freeze in front of the group.

  1. Thursday 10:00, project the week summary: headline ("RM-310A down Thursday, FM-9 Mon–Tue"), list by area, crew load.
  2. Walk the list; edit in place what the room changes (move a job, drop one, add one from the backlog). The blockers of A appear inline in red next to the row.
  3. Check the overtime lines and say why they stay (the reason is typed here and travels to the KPI page).
  4. Press Freeze in front of the group: snapshot stored, lock badge, the summary becomes the printed minutes of the meeting.
  5. After the meeting, the week board and the daily plans read from the frozen list; the KPI page starts computing 5.4.3 / 5.4.4 for week 39 as CATS hours arrive.

C

The week ledger: freeze as an event, then every change on record with its reason

The screen is the history of the week: draft, frozen, and every change after the freeze as a line with who, when and why. Added after freeze = break-in with reason; removed = deferred with reason; moved = moved. Next to it, the frozen list against the current list, so on Friday the Schedule Compliance figure has no surprises.

  1. Thursday: press Freeze (same checks as A, shown as a short list). The event goes on the ledger with the snapshot.
  2. From Monday, adding an order to the week asks for a reason (Production, Mechanical, Electrical, Good) and marks it as break-in; removing one asks for a deferral reason; moving between days is just recorded.
  3. The frozen vs current table shows each order's frozen hours, current hours and CATS actuals as they arrive.
  4. The compliance preview at the top updates daily: scheduled hours worked / frozen hours (5.4.3) and frozen orders touched / frozen orders (5.4.4).
  5. At week close (screen 4) the ledger is already the list of break-ins with reasons; nothing to type again.

In all three: the freeze stores the same snapshot (orders, hours per day, crew, capacity) and that snapshot is the only thing the Schedule Compliance KPIs read; the freeze can be redone until Sunday night with a reason; from Monday every addition is a break-in and every removal a deferral, each with a reason; and the checks are the same (order present, estimated hours present, parts status, crew load, SAP week marker), only where they are shown changes.

The practical difference. A is a gate (check, then lock, then meet). B is the meeting itself (meet, adjust, lock in front of everyone). C is a ledger (lock, then watch every change with its reason). They stack naturally: A's checks appear inline in B and as a short list in C; C is what week close (screen 4) reads. The real choice is when the lock happens: before the meeting, at the end of it, or as an event you keep amending on record.
