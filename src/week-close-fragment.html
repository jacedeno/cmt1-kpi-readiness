Week close: three ways to lay it out

Screen 4. Once the CATS hours of the week are in SAP, the week in transit is closed: the tool compares the frozen list with what was actually worked, asks the planner only for what SAP cannot know (the reason behind each break-in, each frozen order not worked, the overtime, the contractor hours, the week's headline) and writes the week's KPI record. This replaces the KPI tab and the paste into the KPI web app. Example: week 38 (14–18 Sep), closed Thursday 24 Sep from the CATS_DA export of that morning.

A

The answer queue: only what needs a human, one click each

The tool does the comparison and shows a queue of the items it cannot decide alone: each break-in needs a reason, each frozen order not worked needs a reason, the overtime needs its explanation confirmed, the contractor hours need a number. A progress bar counts the answers; Close week unlocks when the queue is empty. The KPI card below updates as answers come in.

  1. Upload the morning's CATS_DA and ZEMR031 closed exports. The tool matches hours to the frozen list of week 38.
  2. The queue lists break-ins (orders with hours in the week that were not frozen): pick the reason, Production / Mechanical / Electrical / Good, one click.
  3. Then frozen orders not worked: pick why (parts, not released by production, no people, deferred to week 39).
  4. Then overtime above 40 h per person: confirm or edit the explanation typed at the freeze.
  5. Then contractor hours (not in CATS): type the week's hours per contractor crew.
  6. Type the week headline and press Close week. The KPI record is written; the KPI page shows week 38.

B

Frozen vs actual, the whole week in one table

One row per order that was frozen or worked, with frozen hours, booked hours, status and a reason cell where one is needed. It is the old KPI tab turned into a reconciliation: every number is computed from SAP, the only typing is in the reason column. The KPI figures sit at the top and change as reasons are filled in. Good for auditing a week and for explaining a number when someone asks.

  1. Upload the exports; the table fills: frozen hours from the snapshot, booked hours from CATS_DA per order, status computed (done, partial, not worked, break-in).
  2. Filter to "needs a reason": break-ins and not-worked rows show a dashed reason cell; pick from the list.
  3. Rows with hours above the estimate are flagged for SMRP 5.3.5 (Planning Variance); no answer needed, just visible.
  4. Below the table: overtime lines per person above 40 h with the explanation, and the contractor hours row.
  5. Headline, then Close week. A closed week stays visible as this table forever; reopening requires a reason.

C

The guided close: five steps in the same order every week

Closing is a ritual with five steps and a progress strip on top: exports, break-ins, not worked, hours and people, review and close. Each step shows only its own question and a Next button. Slower than A for an expert, but anyone can close the week the same way, and nothing is forgotten when the planner is on holiday.

  1. 1 · Exports. Drop CATS_DA and ZEMR031 closed; the tool confirms the week is fully booked (17 people, 5 days) and flags people with 0 h and no PTO.
  2. 2 · Break-ins. One card per order worked but not frozen; choose the reason; the count and hours are shown.
  3. 3 · Not worked. One card per frozen order with 0 h; choose the reason; "Deferred" moves it to next week's board.
  4. 4 · Hours and people. Overtime per person with the explanation; contractor hours; PTO and absences as CATS shows them, corrected here only if CATS is wrong.
  5. 5 · Review and close. The KPI card, the headline field, Close week. The record goes to the KPI page; the week is read-only from here.

In all three: the inputs are the two SAP exports and the frozen list; the tool computes hours, statuses and every KPI; the planner types only reasons, contractor hours and the headline; Close week writes one immutable KPI record per week (5.4.3, 5.4.4, 5.4.1, 5.3.1, 5.4.5, 5.5.8, 5.5.72, break-in count and unplanned hours by reason) that the KPI page (screen 5) reads; reopening a closed week asks for a reason and is logged. The current KPI web app's history is imported as closed weeks in the same shape.

The practical difference. A answers only what needs answering, fastest for the planner who does this every week. B shows the whole week and is the one to open when a number is questioned. C walks anyone through the same five steps and is the one to leave for holidays and hand-overs. A and C produce B's table underneath; B can be the "details" view behind A or the review step of C. The choice is about who closes the week and how often they are asked "why is compliance 78 %".
