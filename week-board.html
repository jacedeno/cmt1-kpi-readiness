Week board: three ways to lay it out

Same information in all three (SAP orders, day, hours, capacity per crew, overtime). What changes is how the planner moves things. Examples use real week-38 jobs. The layout is chosen with the planning team; the three are shown so the choice can be discussed.

A

Backlog and week by day: drag each order to its day

Left: the SAP backlog with filters. Right: five columns, one per day, one card per order with its hours. On top, capacity per crew with overtime in gold. The most visual layout; each card moves between days with the mouse.

  1. Upload the SAP export; every open order appears in the backlog on the left.
  2. Filter by planner group, priority or type (corrective / preventive).
  3. Drag each order onto the column of the day it will be done. The card carries the estimated hours from SAP.
  4. If an order takes more than one day, split it: same hours spread over two or three days.
  5. The bars on top show hours vs crew capacity in real time. Past the limit, the bar turns gold: planned overtime.
  6. On Thursday, press Freeze: the week is locked and that snapshot is what the KPIs measure.

B

Plan sheet: the Excel table, but with SAP orders and totals that never break

One row per order, grouped by area as today, Mon–Fri columns with planned hours. Totals per day and per area are computed, with a capacity row and a "remaining to plan" row underneath. The backlog opens as a side drawer to add rows. The closest to how the team works today.

  1. Open the week; it looks like the Excel: rows by area, Monday to Friday columns.
  2. To add a job, open the backlog drawer and pick the order. A row cannot be typed without a SAP order.
  3. Type the hours of each day in the cell, as today.
  4. Totals per day, per area and the "remaining / overtime" row are computed. No more SUM ranges that stop short.
  5. Freeze on Thursday, same as in A.

C

Crew lanes: Corrective / Reliability / Electrical rows, one column per day

Each cell is "this crew, this day" and shows its hours against its daily capacity; overtime shows cell by cell. The backlog is a side drawer. The most faithful to capacity, and the one that best separates the three crews that today are three blocks of the Excel.

  1. The screen has three fixed rows, Corrective, Reliability, Electrical, and five day columns.
  2. From the backlog drawer, drag the order onto the cell "this crew, this day".
  3. Each cell shows hours vs that crew's capacity for that day. Past 72 h in Corrective on Monday, the cell turns gold.
  4. At a glance you see which crew is overloaded and on which day, and move jobs between days to level the load.
  5. Freeze on Thursday.

In all three: the card carries the SAP order number (no order, no card), the colour marks priority 1 (red) and preventive (blue), and gold is planned overtime, allowed but visible.

The practical difference. A and C are worked with the mouse, dragging cards. B is worked by typing hours, as in Excel. C makes you decide the crew while planning; A and B infer it from the order's planner group. They can be combined: for example B to build the week and C to review it by crew.
