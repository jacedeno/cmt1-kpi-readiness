KPI page: three ways to lay it out

Screen 5. Every closed week leaves one immutable KPI record (screen 4). This page reads those records: the week's SMRP indicators, their trend, and the way back to the closed week behind any number. It replaces the KPI tab of the weekly workbook and the current KPI web app, whose history is imported. Example: week 38 (14–18 Sep), closed Thursday 24 Sep, with the same numbers as the week close mockup. The eleven weeks before it are illustrative, not plant data.

A

The week's scorecard: one tile per SMRP code, trend inside the tile

The page opens on the last closed week. Each headline indicator is a tile with its value, the numbers behind it, the change against the week before, a 12-week sparkline and its target. Supporting measures sit below in a lighter row. The week's headline, typed at the close, is on top. A click on any tile opens the closed week.

  1. Open KPIs. The last closed week is shown: week 38, closed 24 Sep, with its headline.
  2. Read the six tiles. The top border shows the value against its target: green met, gold close, red far. Grey means no target is set yet; targets are set in configuration (screen 6).
  3. The sparkline in each tile shows the last 12 weeks; the dashed gold line is the target. A code that starts late (5.4.4 only exists from the first freeze) shows a single point.
  4. The supporting row shows break-ins, unplanned hours by reason, contractor hours and planned hours against capacity.
  5. Step between weeks with ◀ ▶; an imported week shows only the codes it carries.
  6. Click a tile to drill down to the closed week: the frozen-vs-actual table of screen 4, filtered to what moves that number.

B

The history table: SMRP codes down, weeks across

One row per indicator, one column per week, like the KPI tab spread over time. Each column header shows where the week's record came from: imported from the current KPI web app, imported and recomputed from CATS_DA, or closed in the tool. A code a week never recorded is shown as hatched, never as zero. The last column averages the weeks selected, as the current KPI web app does.

  1. Open KPIs → History. The last 12 weeks are shown; pick a range (4, 12, 26 weeks, year to date).
  2. Read across a row for the trend of one code, or down a column for one week. Cells are coloured only where a target exists.
  3. The column header tells the source: imp = imported record (5.4.3 as Plan Attainment, planned and unplanned hours); imp+CATS = also recomputed from the CATS_DA history (5.4.1, 5.5.8, 5.4.5); tool = closed in the tool, every code.
  4. The average column recomputes for the selected weeks, from hours, not by averaging percentages.
  5. Click a cell: the drawer shows the formula with that week's numbers and a link to the closed week.
  6. Export the table to Excel for the monthly report.

C

One indicator at a time: trend, formula, crews, the orders behind it

A list of SMRP codes on the left; the chosen one fills the page: a large trend with its target, the formula with the week's numerator and denominator, the split by crew, and the orders that make the gap. Built to answer "why is it 78 %" without opening anything else.

  1. Pick the code on the left (each carries its display alias: 5.4.3 is still "Plan Attainment" for whoever knows it by that name).
  2. The trend shows 12 weeks against the target. Hollow points are imported weeks, solid points are weeks closed in the tool; click a point to choose the week.
  3. The formula box gives the fixed SMRP formula and that week's numbers. The formula never changes; the label can.
  4. The crew table splits the value: which crew kept the schedule and which did not.
  5. The gap list shows the frozen orders not worked or worked short, with the reason picked at the close; each order opens in the closed week.
  6. For an imported week the gap list is empty: the old record has hours per crew, not orders. The page says so.

In all three: the page only reads closed-week records and never lets a number be typed; every value is computed from hours with the fixed SMRP formula, and labels are display aliases. Each week carries its source (imported, imported and recomputed from CATS_DA, closed in the tool); a code a week never recorded shows as missing, not as zero. Averages over several weeks are computed from summed hours. Every number drills down to its closed week: the frozen-vs-actual table of screen 4 for weeks closed in the tool, the imported hours per crew for older weeks. Targets and aliases are set in configuration (screen 6). The history since week 40 of 2025 comes from the current KPI web app; in this mockup weeks 27–37 are illustrative.

The practical difference. A answers "how did last week go" in one look and suits the Thursday meeting and the manager who reads a single page. B answers "are we getting better" and shows the imported history exactly as it is, gaps included; it is the view that feeds the monthly report. C answers "why is it 78 %" and is the one to open when a number is questioned. They stack: A's tiles open C, C's points and B's cells open the closed week. The choice is which question the page answers first.
