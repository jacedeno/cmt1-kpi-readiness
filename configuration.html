Configuration: three ways to lay it out

Screen 6. What SAP cannot know and the other five screens need: who belongs to which crew, the capacity rules and the 40 h line, which orders are standing orders, the reasons picked at the close, the target and display name of each SMRP code, and the on-call pair. Only editors change it. Every change applies from a week onwards; a closed week keeps the configuration it was closed with. Example: the crews of week 38. The standing-order rows and the crew change in C are examples.

A

Settings by section: one list on the left, one table per topic

A classic settings page. Six sections on the left; each one is a plain table edited in place. The tool marks the sections that need attention, such as people who booked hours on maintenance orders but belong to no crew.

  1. Open Configuration. A gold mark on a section means something needs attention; here, Crews.
  2. In Crews, each person has a crew, a "counts in capacity" flag and the week the membership starts. The three leads (the planners, including the electrical supervisor who plans his crew) are listed but do not count in capacity.
  3. The gold banner lists people found in CATS_DA with hours on maintenance orders and no crew: assign them or mark them "not maintenance".
  4. Capacity: hours per day, days per week, the 40 h overtime line, and which CATS attendance types count as absence (PTO, HOLI) and which as worked time (REG, call-out CALO).
  5. Standing orders, Reasons, KPI targets and names and On-call work the same way: a table, edit a cell, save.
  6. Every save asks for the week it starts (default: the next week not yet frozen).

B

The crew board: people as cards, crews as columns, the rest in panels below

Crews are columns and each person is a card showing the week's CATS hours. The pool on the left holds the people CATS_DA found with no crew. Dragging a card moves the person, and the crew's capacity bar updates at once. The other settings are compact panels under the board.

  1. Open Configuration. The board shows the crews for the week being planned (week 40); the pool holds people with hours on maintenance orders and no crew.
  2. Drag a card into a crew. The top of each column shows people × 40 h minus that week's PTO, so the planner sees the capacity effect before saving.
  3. Each card can show the person's CATS hours of the last closed week: gold above 40 h, grey on PTO in the week shown (hidden in this public mockup).
  4. Leads have their own column and never count in capacity. Contract is kept per company: the planner requests contractor people for some days or tasks, and the mechanical supervisors type the hours they worked; there is no fixed weekly default.
  5. The panels below hold capacity rules, standing orders, reasons, KPI targets and names and on-call; each opens in place.
  6. Save asks for the week it starts, as in A.

C

Changes by week: every edit is a dated entry, with its effect shown before saving

Configuration is kept as a timeline. The current state is on top; below it are the changes already applied and the changes scheduled for future weeks. A change is written as a short form (what, from which week, why), and before saving the tool shows what it moves: capacity, the overtime line, a KPI.

  1. Open Configuration. The timeline shows the latest changes and any scheduled ones (gold) for weeks not yet frozen.
  2. Press New change, pick the kind: person joins, leaves or moves crew; capacity rule; standing order; reason; KPI target or name; on-call.
  3. Fill what, from week and why. The week defaults to the next one not yet frozen; a closed week cannot be chosen.
  4. The impact box shows the effect before saving: "Mechanical capacity week 41: 320 → 280 h; Reliability 200 → 240 h".
  5. Save. The entry joins the timeline with who and when; the week board and the daily plan use it from that week.
  6. The KPI page reads the configuration of each week, so a crew change never rewrites a closed week.

In all three: only editors change the configuration; every change has a start week and is logged with who and when; a closed week keeps the configuration it was closed with, so its KPIs never move. People come from CATS_DA and the tool flags anyone with hours on maintenance orders who belongs to no crew. Leads are listed but never count in capacity. Contractors have no SAP hours and float between names and weeks, so they are kept per company; the mechanical supervisors type the hours they worked; people are identified by their personnel number, never by name, since two people can share one. A reason or a standing order is retired, never deleted, so old weeks still read correctly. SMRP formulas cannot be edited; display names and targets can.

The practical difference. A is the simplest to build and to read, and fits a page opened a few times a year. B makes the crew decision visual and shows the capacity effect while dragging; it suits a team whose crews change often. C treats every change as an event with a week and a reason, which answers "why did Mechanical capacity drop in week 41" months later, at the cost of one extra step per edit. A and B can keep C's log underneath; the choice is how often crews change and how often someone asks why.
